# Checking a Press Release Provider Before Buying

Use this before paying for any press release service. Look at a few releases they've already published.

## Signs of a link farm (walk away)

- [ ] The page is packed with spammy ads, casino or pill links, or unrelated topics
- [ ] The page or site links out to huge numbers of other pages
- [ ] The "news" site has no real reporters, no about page, and no local or industry focus
- [ ] The articles are all paid releases with nothing written by real staff

## Signs of a real outlet (better)

- [ ] It's a site people actually read, with its own articles and reporters
- [ ] It focuses on a real place or industry that fits the business
- [ ] It clearly labels paid releases as "press release" or "sponsored"

## Questions to check myself

- [ ] Search Google for the release's web address. Is it actually showing up in Google?
- [ ] Go back a few months later. Is the page still there?
- [ ] Are the links marked "nofollow" or "sponsored"? (They should be. That's fine for getting the word out, but it means they won't do much for rankings.)
- [ ] Does a second provider use the same network as the first? Compare their sample outlet lists.

## Questions to ask the provider

- [ ] Can I see a full list of outlets from a recent release?
- [ ] How many of those are real news sites and how many are reposts?
- [ ] How long do placements stay up, and will they put that in writing?
- [ ] Can I see placements from a year ago that are still live and still showing up in Google?
- [ ] Are the links follow, nofollow, or sponsored? (Paid links should be nofollow or sponsored under Google's rules.)
- [ ] Will they explain every tactic before I pay? (Holding things back until after purchase is a red flag.)
- [ ] Do they promise rankings? (If yes, that's a red flag.)

## After buying

- [ ] Don't use "As seen on" logos for paid placements. Only use them for coverage the business earned.
- [ ] If I mention paid placements anywhere, say they were paid.
