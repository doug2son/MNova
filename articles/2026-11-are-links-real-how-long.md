## Verdict

**Mixed.** Keep the questions about how long placements last and the honest rule about "As seen on." Don't believe that thousands of reposted releases look natural.

Why: Asking how long a placement stays up is a smart question, and the source is honest that "permanent" isn't guaranteed. The disclosure rule is right and important. But 2,500 copies of the same release going up on the same day is one of the easiest patterns for Google to spot, and it doesn't look like real news coverage. And holding back the "schema tactic" until after you buy is a sales move, not a reason to trust it.

## In a nutshell

- 2,500 links from random sites isn't natural, but the source says 2,500 from media outlets talking about the business is.
- The source says it has never seen a difference between "follow" and "nofollow" links, and points out that most business directory listings are nofollow too.
- Some providers' placements only stay up for a month. This source says it has negotiated "permanent" placements with most of its network, though it admits that's not guaranteed. It has links still indexed after a year.
- The "schema tactic" to keep placements indexed comes after purchase.
- "As seen on" is only OK if real outlets chose to cover the business. If you paid for the placement, you have to say so.

## What's worth keeping

- **Ask how long placements last.** Short placements that vanish after a month are real, and worth asking about before you pay.
- **"Permanent" usually isn't.** The source admits this. Get the expected length in writing.
- **The "As seen on" rule is right.** Putting "As seen on" logos on a website for placements you paid for can mislead customers. In the US, the FTC's advertising rules require paid placements to be disclosed, and many other countries have similar rules. Only use "As seen on" for coverage you earned.
- **Nofollow links aren't worthless.** They can still send visitors, help Google find pages, and get the business name in front of people. Since 2019, Google has treated nofollow as a "hint," so it may still use them a little.

## What not to believe

- **"2,500 media outlet links looks natural."** It doesn't. Real news coverage happens on different days, in different words, written by different reporters. A press release going out to 2,500 sites at once is the *same* text appearing everywhere on the same day. Google recognizes that pattern and usually treats all those copies as roughly one thing.
- **"Follow vs nofollow makes no difference."** That's one person's experience, not proof. And there's a catch: Google's rules say paid links *should* be nofollow or marked "sponsored." So if a provider's paid links are "follow," that's the version that breaks the rules, not the safe one.
- **Directory listings aren't the same thing.** People build business directory listings (citations) mainly so the name, address, and phone number match across the web. Their value isn't about passing link strength, so they're not a good comparison.
- **Secret tactics after purchase.** If a method is sound, it can be explained before you pay. As noted before, schema can't force Google to index or keep a page.

## Questions to ask a provider

These were added to the [provider checklist](../checklists/vetting-a-press-release-provider.md):

- How long do placements stay up, and is that in writing?
- Are the links follow, nofollow, or sponsored?
- Can I see placements from a year ago that are still live and still showing up in Google?

## Questions or doubts

- Of the "links still indexed after a year," how many out of the total? A few survivors don't say much about the rest.
