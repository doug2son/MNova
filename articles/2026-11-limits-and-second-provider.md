## Verdict

**Mixed.** Keep the honest warnings and the tips for spotting link farms. Skip buying from a second provider just to get more links.

Why: The first half is good, honest advice. Fix the website's technical problems first, and don't trust anyone who promises the number one spot. The second half asks you to buy more press releases to get more websites linking to you. That treats press releases as a way to buy links, which Google's rules don't allow, and the links you'd get are mostly ones Google already gives very little weight to.

## In a nutshell

- A press release won't fix technical problems with a website. SEO starts with the technical side.
- The source promises placements and a range of links, but not a number one ranking.
- The source recommends buying from a second provider to reach different media outlets and get more "referring domains" (different websites linking to you).
- Check a provider's pages before buying. Link farms are easy to spot: lots of spam, and pages linking out to huge numbers of other pages.
- A link from a real media outlet is worth more than one from a link farm.
- Google looks for outside sources that confirm the business is real.

## What's worth keeping

- **Technical SEO comes first.** If the site is slow, broken on phones, hard for search engines to crawl, or missing basic pages for each service, no press release will make up for it.
- **Be wary of guarantees.** Anyone who promises the top spot is a red flag. It's good that the source says so plainly.
- **Learn to spot a link farm.** This is a useful skill no matter what you buy.
- **Outside confirmation does help.** When trusted sites mention the business with the same name, address, and phone number, it helps Google trust that the business is real. That's the idea behind citations and directory listings.

## What to skip, and why

- **Buying more releases to get more linking websites.** "Referring domains" is a number that SEO tools track, and it does go along with higher rankings. But that's because good sites tend to earn lots of links, not because buying lots of links makes a site good. Google's rules say that buying links to boost rankings is against the rules, and that links in press releases should be marked "nofollow" so they don't pass ranking value.
- **"A different provider means different outlets" often isn't true.** Many press release services send releases through the same few networks, so a second provider can land you on many of the same sites.
- **A paid release doesn't really "verify" anything.** The business writes the release and pays to post it. Google gives much more weight to someone else choosing to write about the business, like a local reporter, a trade group, or a partner.
- **Keep in mind who's talking.** The source sells press release placements, so of course it recommends buying more. That doesn't make it wrong, but it's a reason to want proof.
