## Verdict

**Trash the tiers and the keyword links. Keep the idea of a release for real news.**

Why: This method is the kind of thing Google's spam rules name directly. Google's link spam policy lists "links with optimized anchor text in articles or press releases distributed on other sites" as an example of a link scheme. Putting "brand plus keyword" link text in a release that points at a service page is exactly that. Building tiers of releases that link to older releases is an old trick to make weak links look stronger, and Google has spent years getting good at ignoring or punishing it.

The best case is that Google quietly ignores these links and the money is wasted. The worst case is a manual action against the website for unnatural links, which can drag down both the site and the profile's Maps rankings.

## In a nutshell

- Give the first press release about 4 months to work.
- Around the second or third quarter, do a second release, depending on the competition.
- If the business already ranks first, save the second release for something new, like a new service.
- The second release targets that service, with "brand plus keyword" link text pointing at the service page.
- Each new release links back to the older ones. These are "tier 2" links (links pointing at pages that link to you), so "your links have links."
- For bigger campaigns, put out a release every two months, or monthly to speed things up.

## What's worth keeping

- **Tie releases to real news.** A new service, a new location, an award, or a community event is a real reason for a release. That's how press releases are meant to be used.
- **Look at the competition before spending money.** If the business already ranks well, there's no need to push.
- **Be patient.** Waiting to see results before doing more is good advice in general.

## What to trash, and why

| The method | The problem |
| --- | --- |
| "Brand plus keyword" link text in releases | Google names keyword-rich links in press releases as link spam. |
| New releases linking to old releases (tier 2) | This is a link scheme. It tries to make links look stronger than they are. Most release links are already marked "nofollow," and many reposted copies disappear after a while, so the tiers often stack nothing on nothing. |
| A release every month or two on a schedule | Unless there's real news every month, this is just paid link building on a timer. It also costs a lot. |

## A safer way to do the same thing

- Put out a release only when there's real news.
- Link to the service page using the business name or the plain web address, not a keyword phrase.
- Spend the money you'd put into extra releases on getting a real mention from a local news site, a chamber of commerce, or a local partner instead. One real local link is worth more than dozens of reposted releases.
- Make the service page itself strong, and add the service to the Google Business Profile.
