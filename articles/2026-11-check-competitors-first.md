## Verdict

**Keep, with a few adjustments.**

Why: Looking at who ranks above you before spending money is one of the smartest steps in this whole source. Reading total links against linking websites is a useful quick check, and the warning about Domain Rating is spot on. The parts to adjust: links are only one piece of what ranks a business in Maps, and copying competitors' keyword link text can backfire.

## In a nutshell

- Before buying anything, look at who consistently ranks in the top three.
- Use the free Ahrefs backlink checker to see their total backlinks and their referring domains (the number of different websites linking to them).
- Compare the two numbers. Lots of backlinks from only a few websites is an opportunity. If the two numbers are close to one to one, it's a strong profile.
- Aim to match or beat them.
- Look at the link text (anchor text) competitors use, locally and in big, busy markets.
- Domain Rating (DR) is Ahrefs' own score, not Google's. It goes up mostly by getting more websites to link to you.

## What's worth keeping

- **Research before you buy.** This keeps you from spending money on a problem you don't have.
- **The two numbers trick works.** If a competitor has 5,000 backlinks but only 50 websites linking to them, most of those links come from the same few sites (often a link in a footer that repeats on every page). They're weaker than the big number makes them look. Close to one to one usually means many different websites chose to link to them, which is harder to catch up to.
- **The Domain Rating warning.** Google doesn't use DR. It's a handy way to compare sites, but raising it isn't the goal. Ranking and getting customers is.

## Adjustments

- **Links aren't the whole story in Maps.** Google says local results come down to three things: relevance (does the profile match what someone searched for), distance (how close the business is), and prominence (how well known it is, which includes reviews and links). A competitor might rank higher because they're closer to the searcher, have the right main category, or have far more reviews. Compare their profiles too, not just their links.
- **Quality beats count.** A one to one profile made of junk directories isn't strong. Look at *who* links to them. A local newspaper, a chamber of commerce, or a trade group counts for far more than a pile of random sites.
- **Don't copy keyword link text.** Looking at competitors' anchor text is good research, but use it to learn what looks natural, not as a list to copy. Most healthy link profiles are mostly the business name or the plain web address. If you push lots of "plumber in Dallas" style link text, that's the pattern Google flags as spam (see [the second press release note](2026-11-second-release-and-tiers.md)). Busy markets are often full of aggressive link building, so they're a risky place to copy from.
- **The free Ahrefs tool only shows a sample.** It shows totals and a limited list of top links. That's enough for a quick check, but not a full picture.
